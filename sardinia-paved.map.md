# Alghero to Bosa coast road
ride

Worth	great
Curviness	flowing
Views	spectacular

Forty-five kilometres of empty cliff coast between two old towns, with no village in between and the sea on one side the whole way. The road rolls rather than fights — long open bends, climbs over headlands, drops back towards the water — which makes it a road for looking as much as for riding. Griffon vultures nest in the cliffs above it.

# SP13 Jerzu to Perdasdefogu
ride

Worth	great
Curviness	twisty
Views	spectacular

A climb out of the vineyards of Jerzu into the country of the tacchi, the flat-topped limestone towers that make this corner of the Ogliastra look like a desert canyon. The tarmac is coarse and grippy, the bends come one after another, and the plateau at the top is as empty as the island gets.

# SP17 coast road to Villasimius
ride

Worth	good
Curviness	flowing
Views	spectacular

East of Cagliari the shore turns rocky and the road has to climb over it. Bend after bend hangs steep above the water, past coves and Spanish watchtowers, until the land flattens out at Villasimius. Busy in high summer; out of season it is the classic half-day ride from the capital.

# SP51 up Monte Limbara
ride, ride_quest

Worth	good
Curviness	technical
Views	spectacular
Condition	broken

A dead-end climb to the granite summit of the Gallura, 1,362 metres up among radio masts and an air-force station. Ten kilometres, nearly eight hundred metres of height, alpine-style hairpins on a narrow road with no centre line and bumps that arrive unannounced. The reward is the whole north of the island at your feet, from the Costa Smeralda to Corsica.

The panoramic road along the island's southern tip. For twenty-odd kilometres it rises and falls over one headland after another, each crest opening onto a new cove, a watchtower, a strip of pale sand, and it never runs straight for long. Short enough to ride twice, once in each direction.

# SP83 Gonnesa to Buggerru via Nebida and Masua
ride

Worth	great
Curviness	twisty
Views	spectacular

The cliff road of the Iglesiente. It runs high above the sea past the abandoned mine terraces of Nebida and Masua, with the Pan di Zucchero stack standing offshore, then turns inland over the hills and comes back down to the old mining port of Buggerru. Steep drops, big views, and the ruins of an industry all along it.

# SS125 Baunei to Dorgali
ride

Worth	epic
Curviness	twisty
Views	spectacular
Condition	smooth

The stretch of the old east-coast highway that every rider on the island comes for. It leaves the coast behind and climbs into the limestone of the Supramonte, crests at Genna Silana a thousand metres up, and spends sixty kilometres doing almost nothing but turning, on tarmac that grips and with the Gorropu canyon falling away beside it. Heavy traffic was moved to the new road long ago; what is left is bikes, goats, and view.

# SS126 Iglesias to Guspini
ride

Worth	great
Curviness	technical

The mining country of the south-west, crossed by a road that never settles. It climbs out of Iglesias over a wooded pass, drops to Fluminimaggiore, and climbs again towards Arbus, working along the mountainsides past old pitheads and a Roman-Punic temple in the valley. Winding and technical for the better part of sixty kilometres.

# SS198 Seui to Lanusei
ride

Worth	epic
Curviness	twisty
Condition	smooth
Traffic	empty

The road riders come back from Sardinia talking about. It crosses the empty mountains between the Barbagia and the Ogliastra in one unbroken run of corners — well surfaced, easy to read, and with next to no cars on it. Below Gairo it passes the roofless houses of Gairo Vecchio, abandoned after the flood of 1951 and left standing on the slope.

# Villanova Monteleone to Bosa via Montresta
ride

Worth	good
Curviness	twisty
Views	nice
Traffic	light

The inland answer to the coast road, and the way to turn Alghero–Bosa into a loop. A narrow road holds the hillside halfway up the western slopes, runs through cork oak and pasture to the small village of Montresta, then lets go and winds down to the Temo valley at Bosa. Tighter and quieter than the shore, with the sea only in glimpses.
